## I. BRAND AUDIT: WHERE WE'RE GOING

### The Two-Layer Principle

The brand operates on two simultaneous layers:

**Layout layer — simple, minimal, honest.** The website interface is clean chrome: warm backgrounds, generous whitespace, paper-like cards, clear typography. It feels easy to work with and super clear. Buyers drowning in complexity encounter the opposite of what they deal with every day.

**Imagery layer — sophisticated, technological, visionary.** The imagery is dimensional, aspirational, and unlike anything else in healthtech. Point clouds emerging from frosted glass surfaces. Faces dissolving into structured data streams. VR worlds shaped by patient data. The visuals communicate what AugMend is building: a healthcare world that is experiential and shaped by who you are.

The interface is simple. The vision is not.

### What's Evolving

- **Palette:** Retaining Brand Indigo (#1F1C98) and Deep Space (#0D0B3E) as primary colors, adding warm accents (cream, pale pink, lime, orange) to humanize the clinical register
- **Backgrounds:** Shifting from dark-dominant to light-dominant. Dark sections become the exception, not the default.
- **Visual language:** From generic particle clouds to a specific visual metaphor — point clouds on frosted glass surfaces, density conveying completeness, faces dissolving into data. VR environments and avatar imagery stay for platform pages.
- **UI surfaces:** Clean, paper-like cards and containers in the layout layer. Minimal shadows. Warm borders.
- **Imagery surfaces:** Frosted glass materiality — points emerge from a translucent, silicone-like surface with depth. Not flat halftone on paper.
- **Typography:** Under evaluation — moving toward geometric, clinical, and legible.
- **Overall feeling:** Simple chrome, visionary content. "Reading a clear clinical report" meets "seeing the future of patient care."

### What's Not Changing

- Brand Indigo (#1F1C98) remains the primary color — AugMend's signature
- AugMend is still serious, precise, and not wellness-adjacent
- No stock photos, no VR headsets as primary imagery, no consumer health aesthetics
- The brand earns trust through substance, not design flourishes
- Typography hierarchy and spatial discipline remain rigorous
- The audience framework and buyer-order proof sequence are unchanged

### Gradient System

Gradients serve two purposes: subtle warmth in layout surfaces, and dimensional depth in hero imagery.

**Hero Imagery Gradient**
`linear-gradient(135deg, #0D0B3E 0%, #1F1C98 40%, cream/pink ambient)`
Used for: Hero image backgrounds, imagery with dimensional depth. The gradient provides the environment for point clouds and face→data dissolution effects.

**Light Surface Gradient**
`linear-gradient(180deg, #FAF8F5 0%, #F5F0E8 100%)`
Used for: Page backgrounds, section transitions. Barely noticeable — just enough warmth to prevent sterility.

**CTA Gradient**
`linear-gradient(135deg, #1F1C98 0%, #433D81 100%)`
Used for: CTA bars, footer background. Brand Indigo to Soft Indigo.

**Warm Section Gradient**
`linear-gradient(180deg, #FFFFFF 0%, #F2DDD8 30%, #F5F0E8 100%)`
Used for: Warm section transitions. Pink to cream. For testimonial areas, story sections.

### Color Application Rules

1. **Page backgrounds:** Default to Warm White (`#FAF8F5`) or Cream (`#F5F0E8`). Alternate for rhythm. White (`#FFFFFF`) for cards and inputs only.
2. **Dark sections are the exception.** Use Deep Space (`#0D0B3E`) for maximum one section per page (typically footer or a CTA bar). Not for content sections.
3. **CTAs:** Brand Indigo (`#1F1C98`) fill with white text. Hover: Soft Indigo (`#433D81`).
4. **Links:** Brand Indigo. Hover: underline appears.
5. **Cards:** White background, 1px Warm Border (`#E8E4DE`), minimal or no shadow. Paper-like in layout.
6. **Stats:** Large numbers in Brand Indigo or Near Black. Accent stats in Bright Orange or Lime Signal. Supporting text in Slate.
7. **Never** use more than three colors in a single section (excluding text and background).
8. **Indigo is for data.** Reserve Brand Indigo for information-carrying elements (data points, links, CTAs, key metrics). Not for decoration.
9. **Warm colors are for context.** Pink, cream, orange, lime create the human/approachable layer. They don't carry primary information.

## III. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

### Design Philosophy

Two fonts, clearly delineated roles, no ambiguity. The font should disappear — the reader should see the message, not the typeface.

### Confirmed Pairing: Atkinson Hyperlegible + Source Serif 4

**Display / Headings:** Source Serif 4 (serif) — Adobe's workhorse serif. Professional, understated, warm without being decorative. Used for H1–H3, hero statements, stat numbers, pull quotes. Variable font supporting 200–900 weights. Google Fonts.

**Body / UI / Data:** Atkinson Hyperlegible (sans-serif) — designed by the Braille Institute for maximum readability at all sizes. Open counters, exaggerated differentiation between similar characters (I/l/1, O/0). The accessibility angle aligns with AugMend's mission: patients who can't communicate easily, clinicians who need to scan reports quickly. Supports Regular (400) and Bold (700). Google Fonts.

**Why this pairing:** Source Serif 4 brings clinical gravitas to headlines — institutional and precise, like a research publication header. Atkinson Hyperlegible is the body workhorse — designed for the hardest-to-read conditions, which maps directly to AugMend's ethos of capturing what's missed. Together they read as "clinical infrastructure built with care."

**Google Fonts imports:**
```
@import url('https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Atkinson+Hyperlegible:ital,wght@0,400;0,700;1,400;1,700&family=Source+Serif+4:ital,opsz,wght@0,8..60,400;0,8..60,500;0,8..60,600;0,8..60,700;1,8..60,400&display=swap');
```

**Weight mapping note:** Atkinson Hyperlegible supports Regular (400) and Bold (700) only. Where SemiBold (600) appears in the type scale below, use Bold (700). Source Serif 4 is variable and supports any weight 200–900.

### Typography Rules

1. **Display font** for H1, H2, H3, hero statements, stat numbers, and pull quotes only.
2. **Body font** handles everything else — paragraphs, buttons, navigation, data tables, captions, labels.
3. **Maximum line length:** 70 characters for body text. Use `max-width: 680px` on paragraph containers.
4. **Paragraph spacing:** 1.5em between paragraphs. 2.5em between sections.
5. **Maximum two font weights on any single screen.** Regular (400) + Bold (700). Never three. Atkinson Hyperlegible supports only these two weights.
6. **Never use colored text for emphasis** within body copy. Use size, weight, and spatial hierarchy.
7. **Monospaced numerals** in data displays (tabular figures where available).
8. **Generous letter-spacing** at caption/label sizes (0.03-0.06em). Data labels breathe.

## IV. VISUAL LANGUAGE & IMAGERY

### The Two-Layer System

This section defines the imagery layer — the sophisticated, visionary half of the brand. While the layout layer (Section V) is deliberately simple, the imagery layer carries the vision. Every image should feel like it belongs in an exhibition about the future of clinical intelligence.

### Core Visual Metaphor

**"We capture the data points to help you build the full clinical picture."**

Individual data points, collected and structured, forming a complete picture. The points are Brand Indigo (#1F1C98) and the background is like a creamy frosted glass — points blend into the surface as if they merge from it with depth and density. Where data is dense, the picture is clear. Where data is sparse, the picture is incomplete.

This metaphor operates at every level: a hand emerging from data, a face dissolving into information streams, a VR world shaped by patient disclosure. The visual system is not flat illustration on paper — it has dimensionality, translucency, and material presence.

### Visual Treatments

**1. Dot-Grid Portraiture on Frosted Glass (Primary — Homepage, Brand)**
Human forms rendered as deep indigo blue dots on a very dense, very fine regular grid against a creamy frosted glass background. Dot SIZE creates the image — larger dots in dense areas (palm, brow, torso) define the subject, dots shrink toward the edges until they disappear into the background grid. The grid never breaks; even empty areas have tiny visible dots. The frosted glass surface gives each dot subtle three-dimensional depth — like raised pins emerging from the surface, not flat ink on paper.

The hand image is the north star. All portraiture should match its grid density, dot scale, and surface quality.

Applications: Hero images, section accents, brand assets, social media, presentation covers.

Rules:
- Dots are deep Brand Indigo on cream-frosted glass backgrounds
- The grid is VERY dense and fine — small dots, closely spaced
- Dot size variation creates the image, not color variation or position variation
- The grid is regular and never breaks — dots always on the grid
- Background grid dots are Silver Mist (#C4CDD5), very small, visible in all "empty" areas
- Image-forming dots range from tiny to medium (never blobs) — even the largest dots read as individual points
- The frosted glass surface creates subtle 3D depth — dots appear raised, not printed
- Subjects: hands, faces in profile, human silhouettes, abstract forms. Never specific/identifiable individuals.

**2. Face → Data → Text Dissolution (Hero Concept)**
The signature homepage image: a human face in point-cloud style dissolving at its edges into structured text, data streams, and clinical information. The face represents the patient. The dissolution represents what AugMend captures — the transition from human experience to structured clinical intelligence. Information flows outward from the portrait like data being organized.

This is the single most important brand image. It communicates the entire product concept in one visual.

**3. VR Worlds as Point-Cloud Architecture (Platform Pages)**
For Anodyne, How It Works, and product-focused pages: immersive barrel-vaulted spaces made entirely of the dot-grid — like a LiDAR point-cloud scan of an architectural interior. Deep indigo dots on cream trace the surfaces: curved ceilings, receding walls, floors extending to a vanishing point. Human-scale figures stand within for scale, rendered as warm-glowing dot clusters. These represent the patient-facing therapeutic experience — healthcare worlds made of data, shaped by who you are.

The aesthetic inverts a typical LiDAR scan (white dots on black) into the AugMend language (indigo dots on cream). The architecture is made of the same grid system as the portraiture — connecting the brand visually.

**4. Dot-Grid Backgrounds (Layout Texture)**
Subtle, regular grids of small dots as section textures. The grid represents the structured framework AugMend provides. Where data is captured, dots are denser and bluer. Where it's missing, dots are sparse and gray.

- Grid dot size: ~2px diameter
- Color: Silver Mist (#C4CDD5) at 30-50% opacity
- Spacing: 16-24px between dots
- Appears on: most light background sections as a barely-visible texture
- Can be "activated" — dots becoming larger and more indigo where data is conceptually present

**5. Clean Data Visualization**
Thin-line charts, minimal axes, generous whitespace. Data should breathe.

- Primary data color: Brand Indigo (#1F1C98)
- Secondary data: Soft Indigo (#433D81)
- Accent data: Bright Orange (#E8843A) or Lime Signal (#B8D94E)
- Gridlines: Silver Mist at 30% opacity
- Labels: Slate (#6B7B8D)
- No decorative chart elements. Thin lines (1-1.5px). Direct labels on data points, not legends.

**6. Paper-Like UI Surfaces (Layout Layer Only)**
Cards and containers in the layout layer feel like clean clinical forms — minimal shadows, warm borders, flat on the page. This is the simple half of the two-layer system. The UI chrome is plain so the imagery can be visionary.

### What We Never Do

1. No stock photos of doctors, patients, or medical settings
2. No VR headsets as primary imagery (the experience matters, not the device)
3. No bubbly/rounded elements beyond 16px radius
4. No wellness language or consumer health aesthetics
5. No flat-ink-on-paper aesthetics — dots have three-dimensional depth on frosted glass, not flat print
6. No dark backgrounds as default — light and warm is the default atmosphere
7. No visual clutter — if it doesn't clarify, remove it
8. No decorative animation that doesn't communicate information

### What We Always Do

1. Lead with whitespace in layout — when in doubt, add more space
2. Use the dot-grid as a structural element on every page
3. Give imagery dimensional depth — frosted glass surfaces, point clouds with material presence
4. Let typography carry the message in the layout layer; let imagery carry the vision
5. Use warm backgrounds (cream, pale pink) to humanize clinical content
6. Reserve indigo for information-carrying elements (data points, links, CTAs)
7. Use orange and lime sparingly — as signal, not atmosphere
8. Include VR world / avatar / experiential imagery on platform pages
9. Show the face → data → text dissolution at least once on the homepage
10. Ensure every visual connects to the copy — data portals, disclosure depth, world models

### Iconography

**Style:** Line icons, 1.5px stroke weight, rounded caps. Single color: Brand Indigo on light backgrounds, white on dark backgrounds. No filled icons. No multicolor icons.

**Size system:** 24px (inline/body), 32px (card headers), 48px (feature blocks)

## VI. INTERACTION DESIGN LANGUAGE

### Scroll Behavior

The homepage uses scroll-triggered storytelling with quiet, restrained interactions. Content appears with clarity, not drama.

**General entrance pattern:** Content fades in with a subtle upward translate (12px → 0px) and opacity (0 → 1). Duration: 0.5s. Easing: ease-out. Stagger between elements: 0.1s.

**Section-specific notes:**

**Hero:** Light background (Cream or Warm White). Full-viewport. Text is present immediately — no reveal animation. The face → data → text dissolution image is visible or slowly activating. If anything animates, it's the point cloud resolving or the data streams flowing from the face.

**Mechanism (How It Works):** Scroll-locked viewport for the four-step workflow. Steps transition cleanly — content swaps with a cross-fade (0.4s), not a slide. Device showcase (phone, tablet, headset) below the steps.

**Stats/Evidence:** Number counter animations (count from 0 to final value over 1.2s, ease-out). Stat cards enter with a subtle scale-up (0.97 → 1.0) and opacity fade.

**All other sections:** Fade-and-translate entrance. 0.5s, ease-out. Simplicity is the interaction design.

### Transition Curves

| Animation Type | Easing | Duration |
|----------------|--------|----------|
| Fade in | `ease-out` | 0.5s |
| Slide up | `ease-out` | 0.5s |
| Scale up | `ease-out` | 0.4s |
| Counter | `ease-out` | 1.2s |
| Cross-fade | `ease-in-out` | 0.4s |

### Micro-interactions

**Button hover:** Background shifts (Brand Indigo → Soft Indigo) over 0.2s. No scale change.
**Card hover:** 1px border darkens slightly (Warm Border → Slate at 30%) over 0.2s. No elevation change.
**Nav link hover:** 2px bottom border appears over 0.2s in Brand Indigo.
**Link hover:** Underline appears.

### Performance Rules

1. All animations use `transform` and `opacity` only.
2. Intersection Observer for scroll-triggered animations — no scroll event listeners.
3. GSAP ScrollTrigger for the scroll-locked mechanism section only.
4. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` — show all content in final state immediately.
5. Target: 60fps on mid-range mobile devices.
6. No continuous animations except the hero point-cloud activation (and even that can be static on mobile).

## VIII. SECTION THEMING

### Light Sections (Default — 90% of content)

Light sections carry clarity and honesty. The content breathes. The dot-grid is subtly visible. Typography does the work.

```
--bg: #FAF8F5 (Warm White) or #F5F0E8 (Cream) or #FFFFFF (White)
--text: #1A1A1A (Near Black)
--text-secondary: #6B7B8D (Slate)
--accent: #1F1C98 (Brand Indigo)
--border: #E8E4DE (Warm Border)
```

### Warm Accent Sections (for rhythm — 5-8% of content)

Warm sections provide visual rhythm and emotional warmth. Used for testimonials, origin story, human-centered content.

```
--bg: #F2DDD8 (Pale Pink at 40-60% opacity) or #F8E8E5 (Soft Rose)
--text: #1A1A1A
--accent: #E8843A (Bright Orange) or #1F1C98 (Brand Indigo)
--border: #E8E4DE
```

### Dark Sections (Exception — maximum 1 per page)

Dark sections create contrast for a single high-impact moment: a CTA bar, the footer, or one hero variant. They are not the default atmosphere.

```
--bg: #0D0B3E (Deep Space)
--text: #FFFFFF
--text-secondary: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.7)
--accent: #433D81 (Soft Indigo)
--border: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.12)
```

### Section Transitions

Sections transition gently between background colors. No hard cuts between dark and light. When transitioning from Warm White to Cream, the shift happens over ~64px of padding. When transitioning to a dark CTA bar, a clean 1px border separates the sections. Simplicity.

## X. POINT CLOUD ILLUSTRATION SPECIFICATIONS

This section provides technical direction for creating the signature point-cloud brand illustrations.

### Surface Treatment: Frosted Glass
The defining material quality of AugMend imagery. Points don't sit on flat paper — they emerge from a creamy frosted glass surface with subtle three-dimensional depth, like raised pins on a translucent surface. The surface is warm cream, smooth, slightly luminous. Points blend into the surface as if merging from it. This gives the dot-grid imagery a tactile, material quality that distinguishes it from flat halftone printing.

### Grid Structure
- Base grid: VERY dense, VERY fine regular square grid
- Grid density: aim for ~300+ dots across the width of a standard image. The grid should feel like a fine screen mesh.
- Dot size range: tiny (background/sparse) to small-medium (maximum density). Even the largest dots should read as individual points, not blobs.
- Grid is always visible — even "empty" areas have tiny dots on the grid
- Dot spacing is close and uniform. The finer the grid, the better the image quality.

### Image Formation
- Subject is rendered by varying dot SIZE within the regular grid
- Larger dots = denser areas of the image (subject core — palm, brow, torso)
- Smaller dots = lighter areas, transitions, edges
- Dots shrink progressively to the point of disappearing away from the subject
- Grid structure NEVER breaks — dots always align to the grid, always visible
- The frosted glass surface gives dots subtle 3D depth — raised from the surface, not flat

### Color Rules
- Image-forming dots: deep Brand Indigo (#1F1C98) — always
- Background grid dots: Silver Mist (#C4CDD5) at 30-50% opacity, very small, visible everywhere
- Background surface: Cream (#F5F0E8) with frosted glass materiality
- Monochrome indigo for the dot-grid image itself
- Accent colors (orange, lime, pink) for small annotations or highlights overlaid on the grid

### Subject Matter

**Homepage & Brand:**
- Hands (reaching, holding, gesturing) — represent the patient, the provider, the human
- Faces in profile (eyes closed, looking up) — represent disclosure, introspection
- Face → data → text dissolution — the signature hero concept
- Full silhouettes — represent the complete patient picture

**Platform Pages:**
- Isometric data layers — scattered data at top becoming structured at depth (Anamnesis disclosure depth, platform architecture)
- LiDAR-style barrel-vaulted spaces — immersive interiors made of the dot-grid (Anodyne therapeutic worlds)
- The AugMend avatar — warm-glowing companion figure within the space
- Raw-to-structured transitions — organic dot clusters becoming grid-organized images (Two Copilots, gathering → implementing)

**Evidence & Data Pages:**
- Abstract data forms (curves, waves, accumulating columns) — represent patterns
- Clean visualization with the dot-grid aesthetic

### Production Notes
- Produce via Nano Banana Pro (primary), Midjourney, or advanced image generation
- Generate the hand image (IMG-02) FIRST as a style calibration. Iterate until grid density and dot scale are correct. Then use it as a style reference for all other generations.
- For Nano Banana Pro prompts: lead with "A visualization of [subject] made of different densities of points." Include "the points are on a very dense regular grid but their size defines [subject]." Include "creamy frosted glass." Describe colors ("deep indigo blue") not hex codes. Specify "very dense, very fine" grid explicitly.
- The frosted glass depth is what distinguishes AugMend imagery from flat halftone printing — dots appear raised from the surface, not printed as flat ink.
- See 05-Visual-Generation-Prompts.md for the complete Style Anchor and individual prompts.
- Final images: high-resolution (minimum 2400px on longest edge) for sharp dot rendering
- Export as PNG with the frosted glass background included (not transparent)

## XI. EXPLICIT RULES

1. **Light by default.** Dark backgrounds are the exception (maximum one per page). The brand lives on warm, light surfaces.
2. **Brand Indigo (#1F1C98) is the signature color.** A distinctive deep purple-blue. All primary elements use this.
3. **Dot-grid on frosted glass is the signature visual.** Dense regular grid of indigo dots, size variation creates the image, frosted glass gives depth. Should appear on every major page.
4. **No VR headsets as primary imagery.** The experience matters, not the device. VR worlds and environments are welcome; the hardware is not.
5. **Maximum 16px border radius.** Prefer 8px or 12px. Nothing bubbly.
6. **Maximum two font weights per screen.**
7. **No colored text for emphasis.** Use hierarchy.
8. **No stock photos.** Point-cloud illustration, VR world imagery, data visualization, and typography.
9. **Layout is simple, imagery is visionary.** These are two different registers that coexist. Don't flatten imagery to match layout simplicity.
10. **Flat over elevated in UI.** Prefer 1px borders over shadows for cards and containers. Paper-like UI surfaces.
11. **Shadows are nearly invisible** when used. Warm-toned (black-based, not blue-based).
12. **No carousel or auto-advancing slider.** Static, clear, scrollable.
13. **Warm accents at low saturation for backgrounds** (pale pink, cream), full saturation only for small signals (badges, status indicators).
14. **Whitespace is non-negotiable.** When in doubt, remove an element rather than add space around it.
15. **VR worlds and avatar imagery belong on platform pages.** Barrel vaulting, point-cloud landscapes, the companion avatar — these represent the patient experience and should appear on Anodyne, How It Works, and product pages.
16. **Face → data → text dissolution appears on the homepage hero.** This is the single most important brand image.
