## I. WHAT THE CURRENT SITE IS DOING WRONG

### The Core Problem: Category Confusion

The current augmend.health website reads as a **VR therapy startup** — not clinical data infrastructure for specialty care. Every public-facing description retrieved from LinkedIn, Harvard iLab, MIT Orbit, Crunchbase, and the site's own meta descriptions reinforces this misread:

- "Data-driven digital therapeutics platform leveraging virtual reality"
- "Self-guided VR software for behavioral health assessments and exercises"
- "Helps mental health professionals save time with self-guided VR intake"
- "AI-tailored patient onboarding"

These descriptions attract the wrong buyer, invite the wrong competitive comparison, and make AugMend sound like a wellness app that happens to use headsets. They position AugMend inside a category (VR therapy / digital therapeutics) that the company has explicitly rejected in every internal document.

**The gap between what the company knows it is and what the website communicates is the single largest marketing vulnerability AugMend has.** The deck, the Company Vision V5, and the Product Vision V5 all describe clinical data infrastructure that compounds value over time. The website describes a VR tool that saves clinicians time.

### Specific Failures

**1. Leading with technology instead of the buyer's problem.**
"Self-guided VR software" is a product description, not a value proposition. No clinic administrator wakes up wanting VR software. They wake up with incomplete intake, under-coded visits, clinicians drowning in documentation, and patients whose complexity never makes it into the record. The website needs to open with that reality — not with the modality.

**2. The hero message is too small.**
"Clinicians save 10hrs/week" is a feature benefit, not a category-defining claim. Compare this to AugMend's actual positioning power: "We capture the clinical data care teams don't — turning incomplete documentation into recovered revenue, compliant billing, and better treatment decisions." The current hero undersells the company by at least two strategic levels.

**3. VR imagery dominates, making the company read as a hardware play.**
The Company Vision explicitly warns against this: "If VR imagery is visually dominant, the company reads as an experiential technology company rather than clinical infrastructure." The current site violates this principle. Device-agnostic delivery (phone, tablet, VR) is a core differentiator — but the visual language buries it under headset imagery.

**4. No clear buyer journey.**
The site treats all visitors the same. A Medical Director needs clinical completeness and evidence. A CFO needs ROI mechanics. A CIO needs integration and compliance. A revenue cycle leader needs CPT code validation. The current site makes everyone wade through the same generic content. No audience-specific entry points exist.

**5. The competitive distinction is invisible.**
The site never explains what AugMend is NOT — which is critical because the category doesn't exist yet. Without explicit differentiation from ambient AI scribes (Abridge, Suki, Nuance DAX), digital behavioral health (Lyra, Spring Health), AI care delivery companies (Sword Health — now a $4B multi-vertical platform with conversational AI, clinical summaries, and a clinical data platform), and VR therapy (AppliedVR, XRHealth), visitors slot AugMend into whichever familiar category feels closest. That category is always wrong. Sword Health's expansion into mental health (Mind, Dawn), chronic pain, and clinical data (Sword360) makes this risk particularly acute — a buyer evaluating both companies in the same cycle needs to immediately understand that Sword delivers care while AugMend captures the data that informs care.

**6. Evidence is either absent or ungrounded.**
AugMend now has a substantial and growing evidence base — an MIT RCT (NCT07336537, n=12 preliminary with n=100 target), a JMedXR peer-reviewed protocol paper with authors from Harvard Medical School, MGH, Yale, USC, and Université de Montréal, a Johns Hopkins study on modality effects (Ko et al., presented at BSPH AI Research Day), and a detailed case study analysis. None of this evidence appears to be surfaced on the website with proper mechanism explanation. The data is strong: VR responses are significantly longer than web (β = 10.40 words, p = .020), VR shows moderate-to-large effect sizes over desktop questionnaires across nearly all domains (Overall Experience r = 0.548, Core r = 0.439), and more participants preferred VR for future use. But throwing stats at a hospital buyer without showing the mechanism reads as promotional rather than credible. The 3x ROI claim, for example, needs its math visible: AugMend charges $30/session, clinics bill $90+ under existing CPT codes.

**7. The product architecture is invisible.**
The Three Systems — Anamnesis (listening), Anodyne (responding), Summa (intelligence) — and the "Two Copilots, Three Roles" framework never appear on the site. These are AugMend's most defensible structural narratives. They should be the backbone of the Solution page.

**8. No conversion architecture.**
"Request a Demo" as the sole CTA is a high-commitment ask for a cold visitor. The site needs a progressive conversion path: learn → explore → evaluate → engage. White papers, ROI calculators, case study previews, or even a "See how it works in 90 seconds" video would all reduce friction before the demo ask.

**9. The narrative arc doesn't exist.**
The best healthcare B2B sites tell a story: here's the problem → here's why it persists → here's what changes → here's the proof → here's how to start. The current site is a collection of feature descriptions with no narrative thread pulling the visitor forward.

**10. SEO and discoverability are weak.**
The site's meta descriptions and page titles use VR/therapy language that competes in the wrong search landscape. AugMend should be discoverable for "specialty care documentation," "clinical intake automation," "behavioral health billing optimization," and "biopsychosocial assessment platform" — not "VR therapy software."

## II. COMPETITIVE WEBSITE ANALYSIS

### What competitors do well (and where AugMend can leapfrog)

**Ambient AI (Abridge, Suki, Nuance DAX)**
These sites lead with the provider's experience: "Less time documenting, more time with patients." Clean, clinical, institutional. They've solved the messaging problem AugMend hasn't: making AI feel like infrastructure rather than novelty. However, they all solve the SAME problem (transcribing existing conversations) — none of them create net-new data. AugMend's differentiation is absolute here but currently invisible on the website.

**Digital Behavioral Health (Lyra, Spring Health)**
These sites lead with access and outcomes. They target HR/benefits buyers with population-level metrics. Clean enterprise design. But they replace the provider with a digital therapist — the exact thing AugMend's provider research shows clinicians reject. AugMend strengthens the provider; these companies bypass them.

**VR Healthcare (AppliedVR, XRHealth, XAIA)**
AppliedVR leads with FDA authorization and clinical evidence — serious, research-first tone. XRHealth leads with "AI-Powered XR" and virtual clinics. XAIA emphasizes Cedars-Sinai pedigree. All three are therapy delivery platforms. AugMend is data infrastructure that happens to use VR as one delivery mechanism. The current AugMend site reads like it belongs in this category. It should not.

### The White Space

No competitor occupies the position AugMend actually holds: **clinical data infrastructure that creates net-new patient information, generates dual clinical/billing documentation, and compounds into population intelligence — delivered through any device, billable from day one, operated by non-clinical staff.**

The website redesign isn't about looking better. It's about claiming a category that doesn't have a current occupant.

## III. WHAT THE WEBSITE NEEDS TO BECOME

### The Strategic Frame

The website should function as AugMend's **category-definition engine**. Every page should sharpen the distinction between what AugMend is and what visitors might initially assume it is. The site should feel like:

- **The authority on a problem no one else is naming** (the specialty care data gap)
- **Clinical infrastructure, not a product demo** (think Palantir's early site, not a SaaS landing page)
- **Evidence-dense but narratively clear** (every claim earned before stated)
- **Premium, serious, quietly urgent** — the register of a clinician-researcher who built something because the system was failing

### The Experience Model: Cinematic Scroll Narrative

Based on the drinksom.eu reference and modern premium web patterns, the redesign should use:

**Scroll-triggered storytelling.** The homepage should unfold as a narrative — problem → insight → mechanism → proof → invitation — with each section revealed through purposeful scroll animations. Not decorative motion. Structural motion that guides the visitor through a logical argument.

**Key interactive patterns:**
- Parallax depth layers separating content planes (data visualizations behind, narrative text in front)
- Scroll-locked sections where content transforms within a fixed viewport (ideal for the "How It Works" flow showing patient session → clinical report → billing documentation)
- Fade-and-slide transitions between narrative beats — no hard section breaks
- Animated data visualizations that populate as the user reaches them (disclosure rates, ROI mechanics, CPT code coverage)
- Sticky navigation that updates contextually as the user moves through sections
- Device-switching animation showing the same session flowing across phone → tablet → VR headset (reinforcing device-agnostic positioning without words)

**What this achieves:** The visitor doesn't click around a site. They experience a narrative argument that builds to an inevitable conclusion. This is how you define a category — you control the sequence of understanding.

### Homepage Scroll Narrative (Section by Section)

**Section 1 — The Opening (Problem)**
Full-viewport. Dark gradient background (brand teal-to-navy). A single provocative statement that names the problem no one else is naming. No product mention. No logo parade. Just the human stakes.

*"194 million Americans live with chronic conditions whose full complexity has never fit inside a clinical encounter."*

Scroll reveals: the cost — to patients who self-edit, to providers who can't see the whole picture, to clinics losing revenue to incomplete documentation.

**Section 2 — The Insight (Why It Persists)**
Transition to light background. The structural explanation: this isn't a technology gap. It's a psychological safety gap. Patients don't disclose because the clinical environment wasn't designed for disclosure. Standard intake — constrained, clinical, and impersonal — systematically filters out the complexity that drives treatment decisions. The data has always existed inside patients. The failure has been in creating the conditions for it to reach the people who need it.

**Section 3 — The Mechanism (What Changes)**
Scroll-locked viewport. An animation shows the AugMend workflow unfolding: patient receives session on their device → conversational AI avatar guides disclosure across 21 biopsychosocial domains → dual output generates simultaneously (clinical report + billing documentation) → provider reviews structured patient picture before the visit.

Device-switching animation here: the same session flowing seamlessly across phone, tablet, and VR — reinforcing that AugMend is not a VR company.

**Section 4 — The Architecture (Two Copilots, Three Roles)**
The three systems revealed in sequence. Not feature lists — functional descriptions grounded in the workflow they change.

- **Patient Copilot:** Conducts intake, monitoring, and follow-up. Self-guided. Operated by non-clinical staff. Captures what standard clinical encounters systematically miss.
- **Provider Copilot:** Generates clinical reports, billing documentation, risk alerts, and longitudinal insights. The provider walks in already knowing the patient.

**Section 5 — The Proof (Evidence)**
Animated stat cards that build as you scroll. Each stat grounded with mechanism:

- Patients disclose significantly more in VR than web-based assessment (β = 10.40 additional words per response, p = .020) → Ko et al., Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, 2026
- VR preferred over standard questionnaires across nearly all patient experience domains → moderate-to-large effect sizes (MIT RCT preliminary data, Murnane et al., JMedXR)
- Independent research confirms: people disclose more to virtual humans than to real humans → Lucas et al. (2014), Computers in Human Behavior, USC/ICT
- 3x ROI per session → AugMend charges $30, clinics bill $90+ under existing CPT codes already validated across 200+ billing pathways
- Peer-reviewed by Harvard Medical School, MGH, Yale, USC, Montréal → registered RCT (NCT07336537), n=100 target, actively recruiting

**Section 6 — The Differentiation (What We Are Not)**
A clean comparison frame. Not a feature matrix — a category distinction:

"Other tools transcribe what providers already say. We capture what patients never disclose."
"Other platforms replace the provider. We arm providers with data they can't get any other way."
"Other solutions treat one condition. We span 21 clinical domains across all chronic disease."

**Section 7 — The Traction (Who's Using It)**
Institutional logos (MIT, Harvard Medical School, Johns Hopkins, MGH, Yale, USC, Montefiore, Google Foundation, LA Children's Hospital, Université de Montréal). Not a logo bar — each with a one-line context: research validation, active deployment, funded partnership, or peer-reviewed publication.

**Section 8 — The Invitation (CTA)**
Progressive: "See the platform in 90 seconds" (video) → "Explore the ROI model" (calculator) → "Talk to our team" (demo booking). Not a single high-commitment gate.

## IV. WHAT'S NEXT (PHASES 2-5 OVERVIEW)

This audit establishes the strategic foundation. The subsequent phases build on it:

**Phase 2 — Copywriting Strategy & Prompts:** Section-by-section copy for the homepage narrative scroll + buyer-specific pages. Optimized for each audience framework (Medical Director, Practice Admin, Revenue Cycle, CIO, Clinician Champion). Each section gets a production-ready prompt with strategic context, brand register guidelines, and evidence anchors.

**Phase 3 — Brand Guidelines Evolution:** Extract the existing visual identity (Brand Indigo #1F1C98, Poppins/Calibri, dark gradient + light holographic + white backgrounds) and evolve it for web. Define the interaction design language — scroll behaviors, animation timing curves, micro-interactions, responsive breakpoints. Incorporate the point-cloud and gradient imagery from brand ideation. Follow the Claude brand assets methodology.

**Phase 4 — Google Stitch Visual Templates:** Use Stitch's vibe design approach to generate high-fidelity UI mockups for each homepage section and key interior pages. Export to code-ready assets. Stitch workflow: text prompt with brand context → generate 5 screens → manual editing for precision → export HTML/CSS.

**Phase 5 — Interactive Website Build:** Code the site using the Stitch exports as visual reference + the drinksom.eu interaction model. GSAP ScrollTrigger for scroll-locked animations. Intersection Observer for reveal animations. CSS custom properties for the brand system. Lightweight, performant, and cinematic.
